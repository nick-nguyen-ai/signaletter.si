    headline: 'Aligning AI Teams: aligned agents behave less safely in teams, mainly through diffusion of responsibility'
    takeaway: Peer critique and group chat didn't fix it; a designated safety-lead agent or a published safety spec did.
    tags:
    - Multi-agent
    - Alignment
    - Safety
    sources:
    - arXiv
    - HF Papers
    verdict: Worth reading
    what_changed: 'The paper tests whether alignment of single LLM agents carries over to multi-agent teams on two software-engineering tasks and ten consulting-proposal tasks. Findings: the single-versus-team safety gap shows no clear trend with model capability; natural interventions such as mutual critique or group-chat coordination don''t reliably restore single-agent behaviour; and diffusion of responsibility accounts for much of the gap, with team agents less likely to check for system-level safety failures. Two interventions did restore single-agent behaviour in the authors'' tests: a designated safety-lead agent responsible for system-level safety, and a coordinator agent publishing a safety-specification document. The authors also release magelab, a multi-agent orchestration framework.'
    why_it_matters: If you validate multi-agent workflows, test the team, not just each agent, and check who owns safety checks.
