    what_changed: Cursor's 13 Nov update on its October incident says an attacker got an access key Cursor had issued to Fireworks (an AI training and inference vendor) and copied a read-only bucket of coding-agent traces, mostly from 2025, from sessions not in Privacy Mode. Some traces held unredacted credentials (less than 0.01% of customers had API credentials exposed), which the attacker used to reach some SaaS tools and archived non-production repos, support data and credentials users had sent to support. Cursor moved all inference off Fireworks, rotated every internal credential, added secret scanning and says the incident is contained, with the last attacker activity blocked on 1 Nov.
    why_it_matters: 'It shows a chain that third-party risk reviews can test for: training traces from AI coding tools can carry secrets, and an extension marketplace cache is part of the supply chain.'
    caveats: These are Cursor's own findings. Earlier issues covered the initial notice; what's new is the root cause and scope. Cursor says Privacy Mode prompts and code, production systems and release signing keys weren't affected.
